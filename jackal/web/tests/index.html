Arm to the desk

CLICK THE DESK shows the live view full size; click a point of the desk in it. The arm comes over the point at 14 mm and down to 2 mm over the desk -- not over the top of whatever stands there. Anything in its way is knocked aside.

From a cup at a cube's top, as FIND RED CUBE or a grid click leaves it, the arm parks first; clicks after that go straight there.

The point clicked is marked in blue: a crosshair, the cup's rim round it on the desk, a dot. Once the arm is down only the dot is left; once its servos are read, the same in green where they say the cup is, the rim at the height they say. The green rim should sit on the cup in the picture: that tests the calibration, wherever the arm came down.

Find the red cube

Refine the calibration

CALIBRATE's three cubes put the camera roughly in place and lay the arm on it exactly, and are points 1, 2 and 3; the red cube's points all over the desk then make it. SAVE fits the camera to every point's cube, 40 mm each, from the pictures alone, and lays the arm on all the points at once. XY is how far a point's arm reading is from where all the points put it: 0 for any three, something from the fourth on. Z is how far from 40 mm tall the camera sees its cube. One point far off while the others are not: the cup was off the cube's centre, or the cube's edges were not found right. Forget it (×), a cube's too, and record it again near there. Take the three cubes away. Then, again and again, in a new place each time:

  1. Set the red cube down, a corner towards the camera: its top and two sides in view.
  2. Click the grid where the cube stands: the cup comes down 2 mm over its top. Or FIND RED CUBE, to come near it.
  3. Align the cube under the cup with the arrows, watching the cube, not the screen.
  4. RECORD POINT: where the arm says the cup is, then the arm moves away and the camera's snapshot sees where the cube stands.
