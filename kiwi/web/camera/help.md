This page ties the camera to the arm, with the three colored cubes that came with the arm. Do it once the camera
stands where it will stay. Move the camera afterwards, and it has to be done again.

## Place the camera

- Put it anywhere but behind the arm, aimed at the area where the tiles will lie, and as close as it can be while
  still seeing all of that area. From the side, or turned at an angle, is fine. Anything else in the picture only
  takes room from the tiles.
- Look at the desk at a slant, tilted at least about 30 degrees from straight down, not from directly above.
- Keep at least two of the screws on the arm's base, the ones over its suction cups, in view: the calibration
  finds the base by them.
- Aim it with the live view on this page.

## Record the cubes

1. Click the grid near a suggested place for the blue cube (the dashed squares). The cup comes down to just over a
   cube's height.
2. Slide the blue cube in under the cup. Turn it so that a corner points at the camera: its top and two sides in
   view.
3. Center the cube under the cup: the cup's rim equally far from each edge. **This matters most**: a few
   millimeters off here shows up everywhere later.
4. **RECORD** on the cube's row.
5. The same for the green cube, then the red one. The red cube goes on the side nearer the camera.

If the cup is higher than the cube, bring it down with the cube button beside the height slider.

## Calibrate

- With all three recorded, **CALIBRATE**. The arm moves out of the way and the camera takes its picture. Leave the
  cubes where they are until it is done.
- Check the picture beside the button (click it to see it larger): the yellow outlines should sit on the cubes and
  the cyan circles on the suction cups of the arm's base.
- If something is wrong, the box under the button says which cube, and the picture marks it in red. Record that
  cube again, then CALIBRATE again.

Then go on to **Fine Tune**, to make the calibration better with the red cube.

## The camera's mode

Where the camera offers both, **QUALITY** gives the sharpest picture and **FPS** a faster one. Changing it means
calibrating again.

For more, with pictures, see CALIBRATION.md in the demo's repository.
